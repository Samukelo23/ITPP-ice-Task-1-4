About me

Why I'm here, and where I'm headed

Why Information Technology

I chose IT because it rewards exactly the kind of thinking I enjoy: breaking a messy problem into smaller pieces, building something concrete out of them, and then testing whether it actually holds up. Writing a program that runs correctly — and knowing why it works, not just that it does — has been one of the more satisfying parts of my studies so far.

Career goals

My aim is to move into a software development role where I can keep building that skill, ideally somewhere I get to work across the full lifecycle of a system — design, implementation, and testing — rather than just one slice of it. Backend and systems-level work interest me most at this stage, but I'm keeping an open mind as I get more exposure to different areas of the industry.

What interests me in the industry

I'm particularly interested in how well-structured code ages: how a system built with clear boundaries between its parts stays easy to change months later, while one thrown together quickly becomes harder to touch. That's shaped how I approach assignments — favouring classes with a clear single responsibility, and writing tests as I go rather than after the fact.

Skills I'm currently developing

My coursework has focused heavily on Java: object-oriented design, inheritance and polymorphism, working with arrays and collections, and writing JUnit tests to check my own assumptions rather than just trusting that the code "looks right." I'm also getting more comfortable with version control and putting projects on GitHub, which this portfolio is part of.
